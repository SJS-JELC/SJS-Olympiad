# Cross Your Fingers

Require three disconnected diagrams on one canvas: N≡N–O, N=N=O and N=O=N. Each contains 16 outer-shell electrons and gives each atom an octet. Keep one point and the 20-second cooldown.

For N=O=N, each terminal nitrogen has two lone pairs. Oxygen has no lone pairs and donates one pair to each nitrogen, alongside one ordinary shared pair in each N=O bond. Each bond therefore contains three oxygen-origin electrons and one nitrogen-origin electron. Dots and crosses may be interchanged independently for each complete diagram. Charges are not requested.

The oxygen-centred arrangement has formal charges −1, +2, −1. It is a formal alternative connectivity, not a resonance contributor of actual nitrous oxide. The prompt deliberately asks about arrangements permitted by the formula and specifies two nitrogen-centred diagrams plus one oxygen-centred diagram; it does not claim to exhaust every formal Lewis structure.

Chemistry reference: [OpenStax Chemistry, section 7.4, example 7.8](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry/pages/7-4-formal-charges-and-resonance), which compares N=N=O and N=O=N and distinguishes alternative atom arrangements from resonance.

Private marking requires exactly nine atoms and 48 electrons, with one of each pattern. Missing diagrams, duplicates, joined diagrams and incorrect electron origins fail. Coordinates, atom ordering and diagram ordering do not affect correctness. Previously awarded results are not rewritten.
